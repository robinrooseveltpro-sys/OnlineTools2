  5. Copy the CSS variables or download a text report for reference.

What is a contrast ratio?

A contrast ratio measures the difference in relative luminance between two colors. A ratio of 1:1 means the colors have the same luminance; black against white produces the maximum ratio of 21:1.

Hue alone does not determine contrast. Two colors may look different while still having similar luminance, which can make text difficult to read for some people.

WCAG AA and AAA contrast thresholds

For text, WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.3 generally uses a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text at Level AA. Large text is generally at least 18-point regular text or 14-point bold text.

Level AAA under Success Criterion 1.4.6 generally requires 7:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text. These criteria have specific exceptions, so the calculator should be used as a practical check, not as a complete accessibility audit.

Relevant official guidance: W3C guidance for WCAG 2.2 contrast minimum .

Frequently asked questions

What is a good contrast ratio for normal text?

A ratio of at least 4.5:1 is the general WCAG AA minimum for normal text. A higher ratio, such as 7:1, meets the standard AAA threshold for normal text.

Can a color combination pass for large text but fail for normal text?

Yes. For example, a ratio of 3.5:1 meets the 3:1 AA threshold for large text but does not meet the 4.5:1 AA threshold for normal text.

Does this tool check my entire website?

No. It checks one solid foreground and background color combination at a time. A complete accessibility review also considers page structure, keyboard access, form labels, focus indicators, images, and other requirements.

Are my color values uploaded?

No upload is needed for the calculator. The contrast calculation and suggestions are performed locally in your browser.

Why do two visually different colors sometimes fail?

Contrast is calculated from relative luminance rather than hue difference alone. Colors with different hues may have similar luminance and insufficient contrast.

How the contrast ratio is calculated

Each sRGB color channel is converted to linear light. Relative luminance is then calculated using the standard weighted combination of red, green, and blue channels.

If L1 is the lighter color's relative luminance and L2 is the darker color's relative luminance, the ratio is:

(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)

The checker compares the unrounded result with each threshold, so a value just below 4.5:1 is not treated as passing merely because a displayed number looks rounded.
